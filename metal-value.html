Coin Melt Value & Precious Metal Content

See a coin's precious-metal content and current melt value — clearly separate from its collector value, which is usually higher and depends on very different factors.

Metal value vs. collector value

A coin's intrinsic metal value is the value of the silver or gold it actually contains, at today's spot price. Its collector (numismatic) value reflects rarity, condition, demand and history, and is frequently well above metal value for anything genuinely collectible. CoinBids keeps these two figures visibly separate rather than merging them into one number, because conflating them is misleading in both directions.

How the calculation works

Where a coin's catalogued composition is available (for example "Silver (.900)"), CoinBids reads the actual fineness rather than assuming a round number, and calculates fine metal content as:

fine metal (g) = coin weight (g) × fineness ÷ 1000

For silver and gold specifically, that fine-metal figure is combined with a live spot price to show a current melt value. If the catalogued composition doesn't give a usable fineness, CoinBids says so rather than guessing one — an unreliable number is worse than an honest "not available".

Common alloys

Most everyday circulation coins (copper-nickel, bronze, steel, and similar alloys) have no meaningful precious-metal value beyond their face value, and CoinBids doesn't invent a melt figure for these — it's shown plainly as a common alloy instead.
