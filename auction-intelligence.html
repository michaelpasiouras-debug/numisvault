Coin Auction Intelligence

Combine realized auction results with current dealer pricing into a single fair value range — with an honest confidence rating, and live guidance while you're actually bidding.

Realized results vs. dealer asks

An auction hammer price and a dealer's asking price are different kinds of evidence, and CoinBids doesn't blend them together as if they were the same. Realized auction results (which you enter directly or import as a CSV) are classified by how closely they actually match your coin — exact match, strong match, or only a supporting data point — and weighted by how recent and how well-sourced each one is. Dealer listings are treated as current asking prices, a separate and complementary signal, not a stand-in for actual sales.

Fair Value is a range, not a single confident-sounding number, and it's weighted more toward realized results as more good-quality ones become available. With very few or no realized comparables, confidence is explicitly shown as low rather than implied to be certain.

While you're bidding

Enter the current bid, buyer's premium, shipping and any fees, and CoinBids compares the resulting all-in cost against calibrated value/fair/ collector ceilings built from the same evidence — recalculated instantly as the bid changes, without re-running the whole research each time.

Where does the auction data come from?

You add it. CoinBids does not automatically scrape third-party auction archives to build this evidence — several of the larger ones (including some of the most well-known) explicitly prohibit automated harvesting of their data in their own published terms of service. Rather than quietly ignore that or misrepresent where the data comes from, CoinBids simply doesn't do it: realized comparables come from what you log yourself.
