What CoinBids actually does

Identify Coin

Type a coin description however you know it — in English, Greek, with typos, with the year or without — and CoinBids normalizes spelling and transliteration, checks the country/currency/year combination against known monetary history (a currency isn't guessed into existing outside the years it was actually issued), and looks up physical details like composition, weight and diameter from the Numista catalogue.

Coin Value / Price Research

CoinBids searches current dealer listings for your exact coin — matched on country, denomination, year and variant, not just a loose keyword match — and separates the very cheapest listings (useful as buy opportunities) from a broader sample used to estimate a fair market center, so one unusually cheap or expensive listing doesn't distort the picture.

How coin valuation works →

Auction Intelligence

Log realized auction results for a coin (manually, or import a CSV) and CoinBids classifies how closely each one actually matches your coin, weighs them by recency and source, and combines that with current dealer pricing into a fair value range with an honest confidence rating — plus live buy/sell guidance while you're actually bidding.

Metal Value

For silver and gold coins, CoinBids reads the catalogued composition (e.g. "Silver (.900)"), calculates the fine precious-metal content in grams, and shows a live melt value based on current spot price — kept clearly separate from numismatic/collector value, since the two are not the same thing.

Collection management

Import an existing spreadsheet and CoinBids will try to work out, sheet by sheet and row by row, which coins are already in your Collection, which are on order (Pending), which are on your Wishlist, and which have already been Sold — without requiring a specific column layout. Add, receive, and sell coins from within the app afterward, and everything is saved to your own account so it's there the next time you log in, from any device.

How CoinBids actually works

CoinBids validates identity first — country, denomination, year, and where relevant catalogue numbers or variant — before it ever shows a price. Listings that don't clearly match (wrong denomination, wrong year, or things that aren't actually single coins, like replicas or banknotes) are filtered out rather than shown as if they were comparable. Where the evidence is thin, CoinBids says so with an explicit confidence rating instead of presenting a single number as if it were certain.

Is CoinBids free?

You can create an account and use CoinBids at no cost. Sign up to try it with your own coins.

Does CoinBids store my collection?

Yes — once you're signed in, your Collection, Pending orders, Wishlist and Sold records are saved to your account and available the next time you log in, from any browser or device.

Where do auction results come from?

You add them — either typed in directly or imported from a CSV. CoinBids does not automatically scrape third-party auction archives; several of the larger ones explicitly prohibit that in their own terms of service, and we'd rather be upfront about that limitation than pretend otherwise.
